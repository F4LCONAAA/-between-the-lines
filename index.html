Student event coordination memo · Internal notice

About Thursday's arrivals and changes to the check-in process

In light of the fact that, owing to the arrangements put in place in advance of Thursday's scheduled department event, certain details previously circulated may not necessarily remain entirely applicable to each attendee, all participants should please be mindful of the following timetable and of the updates below which take precedence only where specifically described otherwise.

Doors to the building can first be accessed from 10:15, but this should not be understood as the beginning of the event or as the deadline for check-in, and it is not a requirement to be at the desk before the opening time. The introductory presentation itself starts at 11:15, subject to a slight delay for people arriving from other locations; anyone expecting to attend the optional follow-up discussion at 11:45 should confirm this separately with the event assistant.

For participants whose registration has already been verified and who are not waiting to receive a replacement visitor badge, the latest point at which the normal arrival procedure can be completed is 10:45. Although previous messages referred to checking in before the first session, the amended arrangement specifies the above deadline for the final check-in. The desk may continue dealing with unrelated questions after that time, but this should not be interpreted as an extension of participant check-in.

Attendees should bring their invitation email as well as any requested access information, avoiding dependence on an earlier invitation screenshot if one happens to contain outdated schedule information. If a late arrival is unavoidable, speak with the event organizer; the alternative arrangements cannot be guaranteed and should not be assumed to apply to everyone.

What this challenge illustrates

The memo makes you hold several similar-looking times in mind while searching. A countdown adds optional pressure. Real websites should prioritize key deadlines, use plain language and let people control reading time.

From friction to better design

Three barriers. Three better choices.

The experience is only useful if it changes what we design next. Each activity points to an avoidable barrier and a practical response.

01 / WORDS

Reading effort

Barrier: information requires extra decoding and careful re-reading.

Better choice: familiar words, readable typography, meaningful headings and optional read-aloud tools.

An important truth

This was not “seeing like someone with dyslexia.”

People with dyslexia can experience differences in word recognition, reading fluency and spelling, but their experiences are not identical. A symbol code, a countdown and a confusing website are teaching tools, not a faithful recreation of those lived experiences.

The point is to notice an avoidable barrier, then ask: Why did we design it this way—and who might be excluded?
