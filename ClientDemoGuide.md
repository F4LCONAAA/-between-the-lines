# Between the Lines — Client demonstration guide

**Purpose:** Show the concept and invite honest Cardan feedback. This is not a script for claiming the experience has already been validated.

## Five-minute walkthrough

1. **30 seconds — The goal.** Explain that the website is about making avoidable reading barriers visible and translating that awareness into design decisions. Clarify that it is not a literal dyslexia simulation.
2. **90 seconds — Challenge 1.** Decode the revised workshop notice, answering *Room Five* and *Blue*. Point out that the difficult part is added reading effort, not an imitation of someone's vision.
3. **60 seconds — Challenge 2 or 3.** Demonstrate crowded text and optional countdown, or the confusing notice history. Use a hint or turn off the timer to demonstrate user control.
4. **90 seconds — The accessible lab.** Use the **Design lab** link to jump directly to the comparison. Switch to the clear version; enlarge text, increase spacing, and try read-aloud if the browser supports it.
5. **30 seconds — Feedback.** Show the optional local feedback form, its privacy notice and the research sources. Ask for direct criticism.

## Questions worth asking Cardan

- Which activity best communicates a *real avoidable digital-design barrier* rather than mere puzzle difficulty?
- Is the distinction between the artificial challenge and dyslexia sufficiently clear and respectful?
- What accessible-design improvement should visitors remember when leaving the website?
- Does the experience reflect Cardan's **experience + learning** approach? What would make it more useful for an awareness workshop?
- What should be tested next with people with dyslexia and other visitors?

## Evidence to capture (with permission)

Record the actual feedback, what you changed afterward and the resulting evidence. Do not pre-fill or fabricate responses. If someone with dyslexia tests the website, ask about realism and potential misconceptions, not whether the challenge exactly recreates their experience.
